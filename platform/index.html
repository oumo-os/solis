Akello Jane3d ago

The core issue is that current liability frameworks assume clear fault attribution. But manoeuvre-induced debris is different — the actor was attempting compliance. We need a proportional liability model based on intent and outcome.

OK
Osei Kwame2d ago

What about the precedent from maritime law? The COLREGS framework assigns proportional liability in collision avoidance scenarios. Could we adapt that model for orbital manoeuvres?

MT
Mwenda Thomas2d ago

From a remote sensing perspective — tracking manoeuvre intent requires telemetry data. Can we require operators to submit manoeuvre intent filings before execution? That creates an auditable record.

NC
Namugga Claire1d ago

I'd support a graduated liability model: good-faith manoeuvres with debris generation → reduced liability; negligent manoeuvres → standard liability; intentional debris creation → enhanced liability. The intent filing would distinguish these.

Should Uganda ratify the Artemis Accords?
The Artemis Accords represent a framework for international cooperation in lunar exploration and beyond. Uganda, as an emerging spacefaring nation, stands to benefit from joining, but there are sovereignty concerns worth discussing.
EndorsedSarah R. · 2h ago · 4 replies
Space Law Policy Uganda
Replies (4)
OK
Okello Robert1h ago

Great topic. The main concern is Article 10 on resource extraction — it could conflict with our existing mineral rights frameworks. But the cooperation benefits for capacity building are significant.

KA
Kimani A.45m ago

I'd also consider the precedent set by Nigeria and Kenya. Neither has signed yet, and there may be strategic reasons to wait for an African consensus position.

MN

From a law student's perspective — the dispute resolution mechanism in Article 19 is actually quite favorable. It's binding arbitration, which gives smaller nations more leverage than the Outer Space Treaty's vague framework.

Adding a science comm angle — the public perception in East Africa is largely positive. The "Artemis" brand resonates well. Could be a good opportunity for educational outreach tied to ratification.

Why East Africa Needs a Coordinated Space Debris Policy
Okello R., Kimani A. · Journal of African Space Policy · June 2026
Space Law Policy Debris
Abstract: East African nations are increasingly active in space activities, yet lack coordinated policies for space debris mitigation and liability. This paper proposes a regional framework for debris tracking, remediation responsibilities, and shared liability structures. We analyze existing international obligations under the Outer Space Treaty and Liability Convention, and propose a model bilateral agreement tailored to the East African context.
