**The Solarian Lexicon**

*A living vocabulary for the Commons — Third Edition*

> *Projects become coherent because they share vocabulary before they
> share software. The Lexicon ensures that everyone speaks about Solis
> and the Solarian Commons in the same conceptual language.*

**I. Core Identities**

**Solarian**

An identity, not a membership category. A Solarian is someone who
recognises the Solar System as humanity’s expanding home and chooses to
participate in its future. Self-declared — not conferred by any
organisation, not earned through any formal process. Like calling
oneself a scientist, a maker, or an explorer, it describes a disposition
rather than an affiliation.

> **Not:** *a title, a rank, or a credential. No one grants or revokes
> it.*

**The Solarian Commons**

The community. A public commons for people interested in humanity’s
future beyond Earth. Not an organisation, not a platform, not a club.
Claims ownership over neither its participants nor their work.
Organisations remain independent within it. Projects remain independent.
Communities self-organise within it. Less like a corporation and more
like a park — people enter because they know meaningful conversations
happen there.

> **Distinction:** *the Commons is the community; Solis is the platform
> through which it gathers.*

**Solis**

The digital infrastructure through which the Commons flourishes.
Provides the environment for discussion, undertakings, knowledge
sharing, event organisation, and collaboration. The platform, not the
community. Named after Sol, our Sun.

> **Distinction:** *Solis is to the Commons what a library building is
> to the community that uses it.*

**II. Participation**

**Participant**

Anyone who engages with the Commons. The default identity. Participation
implies no formal commitment, no credential, and no approval process.
The Commons does not have members in the formal sense. It has
participants.

**Affiliation**

The relationship between an independent organisation and the Commons. An
organisation affiliates — it does not join, chapter, or merge.
Affiliation means participation and visibility without subordination or
loss of independence. An affiliated organisation retains its own
governance, identity, and purpose entirely.

> **Not:** *membership, integration, or partnership in the corporate
> sense.*

**Chapter**

A term the Commons deliberately does not use. A chapter implies
subordination to a larger body. The Commons has no chapters.
Organisations affiliate; they do not become chapters.

**III. Community Structure**

**Circle**

A community that forms naturally around a shared interest within the
Commons. Not chartered, not approved from above. Self-organising. A
Circle has no formal status — it exists because its participants say it
does. Also used to describe a group of co-stewards holding a shared
mandate: a steward circle.

> **Two uses:** *community circle (informal, participant-led) and
> steward circle (formal, mandate-holding). Context distinguishes them.*

**Undertaking**

A collaborative project with a defined scope, participants, deliverable,
and end point. Any participant may propose one — no approval required.
Undertakings have their own Cell, their own participant list, and their
own archive record. When an undertaking concludes, everything produced
is preserved: what was attempted, who contributed, what was delivered.
The record goes to the Observatory if the relevant steward circle
approves it for public display.

> **Not called:** *expedition, project, initiative (though initiative
> remains distinct — see below).*

**Initiative**

An ongoing effort that invites participation without a defined end.
Unlike an undertaking, an initiative does not conclude — it continues as
long as people sustain it. Examples: a regular lecture series, a running
documentation project, a recurring observation programme.

> **Distinction from undertaking:** *undertakings have scope and end;
> initiatives have direction and continuity.*

**Domain**

Any area of concern around which discussion, undertakings, and
communities naturally organise within Solis. Not a department, not a
committee. A domain is a field of shared interest that provides
structure without imposing hierarchy. Domains emerge from activity and
are maintained by the Knowledge & Observatory steward circle. They exist
at three levels: structured profile domains (life data), knowledge
disciplines (academic and professional taxonomy), and administrative
domains (Commons governance).

**IV. Cells**

**Cell**

A private deliberation space within Solis. Unlike Discussions (open to
all participants) and the Observatory (public), a Cell is accessible
only to its defined members. Cells are where thinking happens before
acting — the working space of circles, undertakings, and task forces.

**Circle Cell**

The private workspace of a steward circle. Contains internal discussion
threads, an automatically maintained decision log, xSTF commission
drafts, working documents, and the full audit trail. The aSTF has read
access to all circle cells as part of its audit mandate. Everything in a
circle cell is recorded. The circle cannot delete its own cell history.

> **Purpose:** *where collective steward decisions are made and
> documented before becoming formal acts.*

**Undertaking Cell**

The private coordination space for an undertaking’s participants.
Contains coordination threads, working documents, task tracking, and the
deliverable workspace. Visible only to undertaking participants.
Archived as part of the undertaking’s permanent record when the
undertaking concludes.

**STF Cell**

The private workspace for a Special Task Force. Contains investigation
materials, deliberation threads, draft verdicts, and evidence. Access
restricted to confirmed task force members. In some STF types, member
identities are partially masked within the cell until the investigation
concludes, preserving independence during deliberation.

> **Archive:** *all cell contents are preserved on conclusion. Cells are
> never deleted — only closed and archived.*

**V. Stewardship**

**Steward**

A participant entrusted by the community with a mandate to act on behalf
of the Commons in a specific domain. Stewardship is almost always plural
— most mandates are held by a circle of co-stewards. There is one
steward role, not a hierarchy of roles. The scope is defined by the
mandate.

The mandate confers recognition. A steward of Astrobiology is the
Commons’ recognised authority on Astrobiology. A steward of Space Law is
the Commons’ recognised voice on space law matters. No separate
recognition act exists. The mandate is the recognition.

> **Not called:** *moderator, administrator, manager, or director.*

**Steward acts**

The categories of action a steward may perform within their mandated
domain: community moderation (conduct reports, warnings, bans),
endorsement (domain-authority reactions to content), content approval
and elevation (approving Tier 2 content for public display, elevating
discussions to articles), publication curation (reviewing and approving
domain publications), approvals and guidance, representation (speaking
externally on behalf of the Commons), and privilege management
(restricting and restoring participant access).

> **Endorsement:** *a steward reaction to content in their domain
> carries mandate authority, not just engagement data. It is displayed
> distinctly from member reactions and carries standing weight for the
> content’s author.*

**Archive**

The Commons’ record of itself. Every significant discussion,
undertaking, cell, decision, and act is recorded and preserved. Not
administrative housekeeping — the Commons’ institutional memory and the
primary mechanism of accountability. What is not archived did not
happen, as far as the Commons is concerned.

**VI. Content Tiers**

**Tier 1 — Freely posted**

Discussions, questions, replies, reactions, domain tags, undertaking
updates, cell contributions, profile updates. Any participant. No
approval required.

**Tier 2 — Requires steward approval for public display**

Articles submitted for the Observatory, public event announcements,
programmes, Commons announcements. A steward with domain mandate reviews
and approves before publication. The steward’s approval is the gateway
between the community and the public.

**Tier 3 — Steward-only acts**

Elevating discussions to published articles, domain endorsements,
curating the Observatory, approving scientific publications, removing
content, restricting posting privileges. Requires an active steward
mandate in the relevant domain.

**Tier 4 — System-level acts**

Triggering jSTF investigations (steward report = direct trigger; member
report = signal only), creating domains, approving organisation
affiliations, issuing official Commons positions. Reserved for stewards
with the relevant administrative mandate.

**VII. Knowledge & Space**

**Observatory**

The Commons’ knowledge space. Entirely public — no participation
required for access. Contains curated sector news, opportunities, a
library of resources, publications produced by Commons participants, and
archived undertakings. Named deliberately: an observatory is where you
go to see further.

**Community vs. Organisation**

A distinction the Commons maintains carefully. Organisations have
charters, defined governance, and bounded purposes. Communities are
fluid and self-organising. The Solarian Commons is a community.
Organisations participate in it. They remain distinct.

**VIII. Philosophy & Orientation**

**Horizon**

The directional concept at the heart of the Solarian identity. Not a
destination, but a direction. The horizon is always receding — that is
its nature, and the reason it works as an organising principle.

> **Usage:** *The Commons is not united by a destination. It is united
> by a direction.*

**Ultra Hic et Nunc**

Latin for beyond here and now. The Commons’ motto. The fundamental
disposition: not content with the present location or moment, always
facing what lies further. Used sparingly — a disposition, not a slogan.

> **Pronunciation:** *OOL-truh HEEK et NOONK*

**Permanence**

The quality demonstrated on 2 November 2000, when Expedition 1 arrived
at the International Space Station and continuous human presence beyond
Earth began. The threshold the project marks. The Solarian future has
already begun.

**The Planetary Map**

A modified Voyager pulsar map that recurs across the project’s
materials. Fourteen radial lines from Sol, positioned as the solar
system stood on 2 November 2000. Voyager 1 is the 14th line — the only
one that crosses the heliopause. Not a formal emblem. A recurring visual
that carries the philosophy: a map with no destination, facing outward,
with one line still moving.
