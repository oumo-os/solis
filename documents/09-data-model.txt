## Cells — Deep Clarification (2026-07-24)

### What is a Cell?
A Cell is a **deliberation/discussion/activity space** — think of it as a chatroom, but each one is unique in its purpose and membership. Cells are where work actually happens.

### Cell Types and Their Lifecycle

**1. Circle Cell**
- Created by a Steward Circle for internal deliberation
- Members: circle stewards + mandated domain participants
- Purpose: discuss governance, policies, domain direction

**2. Deliberation Cell**
The core governance mechanism. A deliberation cell has four components:

1. **Deliberation** — a chat thread where participants discuss
2. **Proposal** — the formal proposal being deliberated, with a link to the source discussion thread if any (or report message(s) for jSTF, or complaint for jSTF)
3. **Deliverable** — the motion, action, or outcome produced by deliberation
4. **Competence-weighted vote** — when all vote and the motion passes, the cell creates/opens an aSTF

**Proposal Origins (how a proposal enters a deliberation cell):**
1. **Discussion thread → steward proposal**: A user creates a discussion thread, participants discuss, and a steward creates a proposal from that thread. The proposal links back to the source thread.
2. **Direct steward proposal**: A steward directly creates a proposal without a preceding discussion thread.
3. **System-bound proposal**: A steward makes changes to a system setting or circle profile, submits the changes as a proposal. When the resolution passes, the approved system settings are activated automatically.

**Multi-circle participation:**
Deliberation cells can have multiple circles participating, not just the originating circle.

**Vote and aSTF:**
When all participants have voted and the motion passes, the cell creates or opens an aSTF for blind adjudication.

**3. Project Cell**
- Projects can have their own cells for deliberation
- Project cells may have different membership rules than circle cells

**4. Motion → aSTF (Adjudication STF)**
- A motion from deliberation spawns an **aSTF** (Adjudication Special Task Force)
- aSTF operates in an **isolated blind cell** — members don't know each other
- aSTF audits the motion in isolation
- aSTF verdict: **Resolution** (motion approved) or **Rejection** (motion denied)

**5. vSTF / jSTF / Periodic Review aSTF**
- All operate in **isolated blind cells**
- **vSTF**: Verification STF — evaluates competence claims, evidence
- **jSTF**: Investigation STF — investigates specific issues (see jSTF section below)
- **Periodic Review aSTF**: Reviews overall health of a circle and its stewards
- All produce **evaluations** (not resolution/rejection)

**6. xSTF → Deliverable**
- xSTF spawns to **execute** a resolved motion
- Produces a **deliverable** per requirements from the **commissioning circle** (the stewards' cell that requested it)

### jSTF — Judicial Investigation Cell

**Trigger:**
Any member can report any member. The report is surfaced as an **anonymous post to the Commons**, visible to stewards. Any steward can sponsor the report, which activates a jSTF.

**jSTF Deliberation:**
The jSTF deliberates on the report in a blind cell. During deliberation, the jSTF can:

1. **Restrict reported user activity** — e.g., suspend steward privileges, limit participation in certain cells, during the investigation period
2. **Select punitive action** if warranted — based on policy resolutions previously passed by the Commons (cited as precedent)
3. **Push a disciplinary motion** — which goes to aSTF for audit

**aSTF Audit of jSTF Disciplinary Motion:**
- aSTF audits the **decision**, not the members
- aSTF **never sees jSTF members** — complete separation
- aSTF reviews whether the disciplinary action is proportionate and consistent with Commons policy

**Resolution:**
When the disciplinary motion passes aSTF audit, the resolution can be:

1. **System-bound**: Remove from circle, trigger fresh vSTF, freeze Ws for a set time, among others
2. **Non-system disciplinary**: Applied as per policy resolutions passed before, cited as precedent

### Flow Summary

```
Discussion → Proposal (steward-sponsored) → Deliberation Cell → Motion → aSTF → Resolution
      ↑                                                        ↓
      └──── Link to source thread ────────────────────────── Vote (competence-weighted)
                                                                      ↓
                                                              Resolution passes → opens aSTF

jSTF: Report → Anonymous post → Steward sponsors → jSTF Cell (blind)
                                                          ↓
                                                   Activity restriction (if needed)
                                                          ↓
                                                   Disciplinary motion → aSTF audit
                                                          ↓
                                                   System-bound resolution (circle removal, vSTF trigger, Ws freeze)
                                                   OR non-system resolution (policy citation)

Periodic Review aSTF → Evaluates circle health + steward performance
vSTF → Evaluates competence claims
xSTF → Executes resolved motions → Deliverable
```

### Key Principles
- **Blind isolation**: STF cells (aSTF, vSTF, jSTF, xSTF) operate blind — members don't know each other's identities during deliberation
- **Steward sponsorship required**: Not just anyone can propose — a steward must sponsor it
- **Domain mandate boundaries**: Cells only operate within the domains the responsible circle has mandate over
- **Each cell is unique**: Different membership, different purpose, different rules
- **Anonymous reporting**: jSTF reports are anonymous to protect reporters; sponsors are stewards who take responsibility
- **Proportionate discipline**: jSTF can restrict activity during investigation but must cite policy precedent for punitive action
- **aSTF independence**: aSTF never sees jSTF members — audits the decision, not the people
